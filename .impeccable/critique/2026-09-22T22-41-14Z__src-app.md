## Overall Impression

The public experience is structurally understandable and the booking choice is well bounded, but the product begins defensively, then leaves newcomers with a blank question field. The dashboard reports broadly instead of helping an operator decide. The biggest opportunity is to turn AIDA from a generic chatbot with disclaimers into a guided financial-learning service with explicit, reassuring next actions.

## What's Working

1. Three clearly labelled sidebar destinations, visible active states, and a mobile drawer give the core app reliable navigation.
2. The guide separates education from investment advice and its hero plus anchor chips create a usable macro-structure.
3. The booking form uses four concrete topic cards with descriptions, keeping its central decision within a manageable four-option choice.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Consent appears coercive before trust has been earned

**What:** The first screen is a dense, scrollable liability wall with a single “Accetta” action. The shared dialog renders a close icon but the controlled state has no exit handler.

**Why it matters:** A first-time financial learner is asked to accept legal risk before understanding the product value; an apparent but ineffective exit undermines trust.

**Fix:** Replace the wall with a plain-language “what AIDA can/can’t do” summary, link full terms, add a clear acknowledgement, and either remove the X or make it return to a real safe state.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

### [P1] Empty chat has no designed first action

**What:** After onboarding, the user sees logos and “Hai una domanda?” but no safe topics, examples, or route into learning.

**Why it matters:** Newcomers must invent both an appropriate financial topic and wording at the moment the product should reduce anxiety.

**Fix:** End onboarding with 3–4 scope-safe starters (budget, risk, debt, mortgage) plus “Chiedi con parole tue”; retain a quiet prompt rail in empty chat.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

### [P1] Booking hides the remaining work and underspecifies the commitment

**What:** The CTA remains disabled until five prerequisites are met, but it does not name the incomplete requirements until a submit attempt. It repeats “free, 30 minutes” without stating the response time, contact method, privacy expectation, or next step.

**Why it matters:** An inert CTA raises abandonment at a high-trust conversion moment, especially on mobile.

**Fix:** Show real-time “Completa 5 campi per inviare” progress; group personal details and topic choice; add “Cosa succede dopo” with an accurate SLA, privacy note, and non-advisory boundary. Correct `Fiscalita` and other missing accents.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] Dashboard is a report dump rather than a decision surface

**What:** Filters, exports, eight KPI cards, chart summaries, charts, appointments, and chat review all compete without one dominant operating question.

**Why it matters:** Operators have to construct the answer from raw metrics; mobile merely stacks the same density.

**Fix:** Lead with one decision (“Cosa richiede revisione?”), prioritise trend/negative-feedback/conversion, demote secondary metrics, and add date presets plus explicit Apply/Reset and refresh status.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Primary controls and language metadata exclude some users

**What:** The icon-only send control has no accessible name; the offline-info trigger is a non-focusable clickable span with a 4px icon; the document uses `lang="en"` for an Italian interface.

**Why it matters:** Keyboard and screen-reader users may not reach critical actions or hear the interface correctly.

**Fix:** Add `aria-label="Invia messaggio"` and focus styles, use a ≥44px semantic button for offline info, change to `lang="it"`, and announce relevant state changes.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

- The offline header copy uses a double exclamation; its detail is inaccessible by keyboard.
- The intro duplicates navigation via close, dots, back, and next for only three slides.
- The protected dashboard login does not identify the intended operator or offer a recovery/support route.
